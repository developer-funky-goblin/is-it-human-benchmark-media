The smell in the kitchen was somewhere between charred wood and sulfur, a sharp, acrid tang that set the back of Mina's throat on fire. She'd been careful -- salt lines drawn twice, the candles lit in the right order, the old words spoken exactly as the book demanded. And yet here she was, staring at a shape coalescing out of the smoke above her stove, twice as tall as the ceiling should have allowed, folding itself into the room like it had always belonged there.

"You summoned wrong," the demon said, sounding almost bored. "This isn't even a proper circle. It's a pentagram drawn in spice rack contents."

Mina looked down. Cinnamon, yes. She'd run out of chalk.

"I panicked," she admitted.

The demon settled onto her kitchen counter, somehow without knocking anything over, and regarded her with an expression that might have been amusement if demons had expressions humans could read easily. "Most do. What do you want?"

"I don't actually know anymore. I just wanted to talk to someone who wasn't going to lie to me."

"Ah." The demon's voice softened, if such a thing was possible. "That's rarer than you'd think, even among my kind who cannot lie at all."

They talked until dawn, about nothing important and everything that mattered, and when the sun rose the demon was gone, and the cinnamon on the floor had arranged itself, unmistakably, into the shape of a heart.
